# What a minute of Stacy costs

In USD and INR, per minute, per call, and per thousand calls — with what it
would cost built differently.

Every table here is generated by [`scripts/cost_model.py`](../scripts/cost_model.py).
Change an assumption and re-run it rather than editing a number into this
file:

```
python scripts/cost_model.py --inr 94.0 --minutes 3.5
```

> **Read the confidence column before quoting anything.** The Anthropic model
> prices are vendor rates. Everything else is a third-party tracker or a
> competitor's comparison table, because the vendors put their real numbers
> behind a sales conversation. Treat the totals as the right order of
> magnitude and the right *shape*, not as a quote. Section 7 lists what to
> confirm before any of this goes in front of a customer.

## 3. The four changes, in order of what they're worth

### B. Claude Haiku 4.5 → Haiku 5.5 — *do this one regardless*

| | input / MTok | output / MTok |
|---|---|---|
| Haiku 4.5 (today) | $1.00 | $5.00 |
| **Haiku 5.5** | **$0.10** | **$0.50** |

**Ten times cheaper, and it is the newer model** — this is not a quality
downgrade to save money, it is the current generation of the same tier. It
takes the LLM line from $0.0045 to $0.00045 a minute.

It saves only 8% of the total, but it is a model-string change, so the
effort-to-saving ratio is unbeatable. Two things to check before shipping it:
Haiku 5.5 uses adaptive thinking and rejects `budget_tokens`, and the repo
still pins Haiku 4.5 whose retirement was announced as "not sooner than
15 Oct 2026" — a date that has now passed. **This change is becoming
mandatory anyway.** Re-run `scripts/bench_models.py` from inside the worker
to confirm first-word latency before switching; cheaper is not useful if it
costs 200 ms.

### C. ElevenLabs Flash → Deepgram Aura-2 — *the biggest single saving*

$50/M characters → $30/M characters, worth **$0.008/min, 16% of the whole
bill**. It is the largest lever on the list and the one with a real
trade-off: voice quality is the product's first impression, and Casey's voice
is a tenant-level decision that has already been tuned. Aura-2's published
time-to-first-byte is sub-200 ms against ElevenLabs Flash's ~120 ms measured
on real calls, so this costs latency as well.

**Don't do this blind.** Run both through `scripts/diff_against_elevenlabs.py`
and listen before deciding. A cheaper voice that makes callers hang up is the
most expensive change on this page.

### D. STT promotional rate and more desk coverage — *small, free*

Deepgram's promotional streaming rate ($0.0048 vs $0.0077) and pushing the
desk from 40% to 60% of turns together save another 6%. The desk work is
free money in cost terms — it is also the thing that makes scripted turns
answer in 0.4 s instead of 1.1 s, so it pays twice. The promo rate is not a
decision, just something to confirm is still applied.

### E. Self-host the transport — *the second-biggest, and the most work*

LiveKit Cloud's agent-session and SIP minutes are **27% of the bill** and
buy no intelligence — they are the pipe. Self-hosting takes it to roughly
$0.002/min amortised, worth **$0.012/min**.

The honest cost: you take on the worker fleet, SIP signalling, scaling,
and on-call. At low volume the cloud is cheaper than your time; the
cross-over is somewhere in the low thousands of minutes a month. This is a
scale decision, not an optimisation.

## 4. What's already been saved

Two changes made before anyone drew up a cost table are worth more than
everything in §3 except the transport move. A table of what's left to do
makes them invisible, so:

| Already done | Saves per minute |
|---|---|
| **Prompt caching** — the stable prefix is 99.2% of a 7,486-token prompt | **$0.0188 / ₹1.82** |
| **The desk** — 40% of turns answered in code, never reaching a model | $0.0030 / ₹0.29 |

Prompt caching alone saves **four times more than switching to Haiku 5.5
does**. Without it, the same call on Haiku 4.5 would cost $0.070/min instead
of $0.051 — the prompt would be the largest line in the table by a distance.

That is the real lesson of this document: **the architecture decisions
already made moved the cost more than the vendor swaps still available.**

## 5. The usage model

What one minute of connected call is assumed to consume. Measured where it
can be; stated and arguable where it can't.

| Input | Value | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Agent turns per minute | 4.7 | assumed — a turn every ~13 s |
| Share answered by the desk | 40% | **measured**, live-call logs |
| System prompt | 7,486 tokens | **measured** — `render_system_prompt(millbrook)` |
| Cacheable prefix | 99.2% | **measured** — a test fails if anything per-call is added above it |
| History replayed per request | 600 tokens | assumed, mid-call average |
| Output per reply | 40 tokens | assumed — receptionist lines are short |
| TTS characters per minute | 400 | assumed — agent speaks ~40% of the call at ~150 wpm |
| STT minutes per call minute | 1.0 | the caller's audio streams throughout |

Cache arithmetic: reads bill at 0.1× the input rate, writes at 1.25×. With a
99.2% prefix, almost the whole prompt bills at a tenth.

**The assumption most likely to be wrong is TTS characters per minute.** It
drives the largest line in the bill, and it comes from a speaking-rate
estimate rather than a measurement. One instrumented call would settle it —
sum the characters sent to the TTS node and divide by call duration.

## 6. India

Two things change, and one of them is a blocker.

**Twilio does not offer voice-enabled numbers in India.** Their India page
says so directly, and lists termination from $0.0456/min — thirteen times the
US inbound rate used above. A real India deployment needs an Indian provider
(Exotel, Plivo, Ozonetel) rather than the stack priced here, and I have no
quotes for those: they are sales conversations, not rate cards.

**The regulatory layer is architecture, not paperwork.** DLT header and
template registration, DND scrubbing at *dial time* rather than
campaign-queue time, and in-country call control that compliance teams
audit. None of that is in this model. It is also mostly an outbound concern;
Stacy is inbound, which is the easier side.

So: treat §1 as US economics. The INR column is a currency conversion, not
an India price.

## 8. What I'd actually do

1. **Switch to Haiku 5.5 now.** Ten times cheaper, newer model, and Haiku
   4.5's retirement window has already opened. Bench first-word latency
   before and after.
2. **Measure TTS characters per minute on one real call.** It decides whether
   the biggest line in this document is right.
3. **A/B the voice** before touching it. 16% is a lot, and so is a caller's
   first impression.
4. **Leave the transport alone** until volume justifies the operational load.
5. **Don't price India off this page.** Get Exotel and Plivo quotes.

At today's numbers, a thousand three-minute calls a month costs
**$153 / ₹14,749**. Doing items 1 and 3 takes it to about **$117 / ₹11,255**.
All five takes it to **$71 / ₹6,898**.

## 9. Beating a competitor at ₹4/min

A company selling voice agents on a wrapper quotes **₹4/min** ($0.0415).
Can we beat it?

**First: that is a price, and everything above this section is a cost.**
Comparing them directly is the mistake that kills margin. At their price,
against our cost:

| Stack | our cost | margin at ₹4 | per 1,000 3-min calls |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Today | ₹4.92 | **−₹0.92 (−23%)** | −₹2,749 **loses money** |
| B. Haiku 5.5 | ₹4.52 | −₹0.52 (−13%) | −₹1,571 **loses money** |
| C. + Aura-2 voice | ₹3.75 | +₹0.25 (6%) | +₹745 |
| D. + promo STT, 60% desk | ₹3.46 | +₹0.54 (14%) | +₹1,628 |
| E. + self-hosted transport | ₹2.30 | **+₹1.70 (43%)** | +₹5,102 |

Regenerate with `python scripts/cost_model.py --target-inr 4.0`, and add
`--target-excludes-telephony` if their price is platform-only and the
customer brings their own trunk (that lifts every margin by ~₹0.33).

**So: yes, from option C onward — but 6% gross is not a business.** That is
before support, your own time, failed calls, retries and the free tier you
will inevitably give someone. Only **E has a real business in it**, and E
means owning the worker fleet and SIP signalling.

### Where their ₹4 could be coming from

A wrapper pays the same rack rates we do, so if they are genuinely selling
at ₹4 all-in, one of these is true:

- **Volume discounts.** Committed-use deals with Deepgram and ElevenLabs cut
  30–50% off list. This is the most likely answer, and it is the one we
  cannot match at low volume.
- **Cheaper components** already — a budget TTS, a smaller model, or
  self-hosted transport.
- **Subsidy.** Priced for growth, funded by someone else.
- **It is not really ₹4.** Setup fee, monthly minimum, overage rate, or a
  number quoted to you that is not the list price.

### Ask these before believing the number

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| All-in, or platform-only with telephony extra? | Worth ~₹0.33/min here, more on Indian trunks |
| **Per-second billing, or rounded up to the minute?** | The big one — see below |
| Minimum monthly commitment, setup fee, overage rate? | Turns a per-minute price into a real bill |
| Is the LLM/STT/TTS passthrough at cost, or marked up? | Tells you whether they have volume deals |

### The rounding point, which is a real edge

Our cost is **per second**: LiveKit meters agent sessions in 1-second
increments with a 10-second minimum, Deepgram bills per second of audio,
and the model bills per token. Most platforms round **up to the minute**.

On a receptionist line that matters more than it sounds, because a real
inbound line is full of short calls — wrong numbers, hang-ups, "are you open
Saturday?".

| A 20-second wrong number | Cost / price |
|---|---|
| Us, stack A | ₹1.64 |
| Us, stack E | ₹0.77 |
| Them, if rounded up to one minute | ₹4.00 |

A line with many short calls can cost half what a per-minute-rounded
competitor charges, at the same headline rate.

### The honest strategic read

**Do not fight a wrapper on price.** If they are a wrapper and we are a
wrapper, the floor is the same vendor rate card and the winner is whoever
buys more minutes — which is them, not us. Price is the one axis where
being smaller is a structural disadvantage.

It is also worth being straight about the desk here: 40% of turns answered
in code sounds like a cost moat, and it is not. It saves ₹0.29/min, because
the model is only 9% of the bill. **The desk is a latency feature** — 0.4 s
instead of 1.1 s — that happens to save a little money. Sell it as speed.

What is actually defensible is in
[features-and-fixes.md](features-and-fixes.md): a correction after booking
that would have left a technician driving to the wrong address, a booking
that moved on a caller's stale "yes", a tool failure that gave the caller
dead air, a farewell detector that would have hung up on someone saying
their name. An HVAC owner does not lose sleep over ₹0.50 a minute. They lose
it over a van sent to the wrong house.

So the answer to "can we beat ₹4" is: **yes, down to ₹2.30 with margin, and
we can quote per-second billing against their rounding — but that is a
tactical win, and the reason to buy is correctness, not price.**
